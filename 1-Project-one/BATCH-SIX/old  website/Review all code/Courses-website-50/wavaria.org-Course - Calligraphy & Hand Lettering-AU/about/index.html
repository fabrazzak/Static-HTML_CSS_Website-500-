Our Story

Where Tradition Meets Modern Expression

Founded in 2020 by master calligrapher Isabella Montague, Wavaria began as a small studio offering in-person workshops in Adelaide. What started as a passion project quickly grew into a thriving community of lettering enthusiasts.

As demand for our courses expanded beyond our local area, we transitioned to online instruction, allowing us to share the art of calligraphy with students worldwide while maintaining the personal touch of our in-person workshops.

Today, Wavaria has taught over 10,000 students across 45 countries, with our courses being recognized as some of the most comprehensive and accessible in the field of calligraphy and hand lettering education.

A Brief History of Calligraphy

The Timeless Art of Beautiful Writing

Calligraphy has been an essential form of communication and artistic expression for thousands of years, evolving across cultures and civilizations.

From ancient scribes to medieval monks, from Renaissance humanists to modern artists, calligraphy has preserved knowledge, documented history, and created beauty.

Today, in our digital age, calligraphy and hand lettering have experienced a resurgence as people seek meaningful, tactile creative outlets and personalized expressions.

Ancient Origins

3000 BCE - 500 CE

Early writing systems develop in Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica, laying foundations for calligraphic traditions.

Medieval Manuscripts

500 - 1500 CE

Monastic scribes preserve classical knowledge through illuminated manuscripts, perfecting scripts like Carolingian and Gothic.

Renaissance Humanism

1400 - 1600 CE

Humanist scholars revive classical scripts, leading to the development of Italic and foundational hand.

Copperplate Era

1700 - 1900 CE

Engraving technology popularizes Copperplate script, becoming the standard for business and personal correspondence.

Arts & Crafts Movement

Late 1800s

William Morris and others revive interest in handcrafted arts, including calligraphy, as a reaction to industrialization.

Modern Revival

2000 - Present

Digital age sparks renewed interest in handmade lettering, with modern calligraphy and brush lettering gaining popularity worldwide.
